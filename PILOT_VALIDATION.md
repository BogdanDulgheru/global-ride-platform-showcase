# Global Ride — Pilot Validation Framework

The purpose of a pilot is not to maximize registrations. It is to determine whether a concentrated ride-hailing marketplace can become useful and economically sustainable.

## Pilot principle

Start narrow enough that supply density can be observed. Expanding geography before reliable local availability is demonstrated would make the marketplace look larger while making the user experience weaker.

## Hypotheses to test

### Supply

- Can active drivers/operators be recruited without unsustainable incentives?
- Does the commercial proposition change willingness to join, stay online or complete rides?
- How much participating supply is genuinely available rather than merely registered?

### Demand

- Can passengers obtain a ride within an acceptable service window?
- Do first-time passengers return?
- Which journeys create repeatable demand rather than one-off curiosity?

### Marketplace

- Are enough requests matched and accepted to keep both sides engaged?
- Does geographic concentration improve availability and utilization?
- Can supply and demand grow without permanently subsidizing both sides?

### Economics

- Does revenue per active participant cover variable operating burden?
- What acquisition and support costs emerge in real use?
- Does the proposed platform-cost structure remain sustainable across different activity levels?

The private operating dashboard should track at minimum:

| Supply | driver retention |
| Marketplace | ride requests |
| Marketplace | acceptance rate |

| Demand | active passengers |

| Economics | platform revenue per active driver |
| Economics | variable cost per completed ride |

| Growth | driver acquisition cost |

## Decision gates

### Gate 1 — Product reliability

The ride lifecycle must work consistently enough that software defects do not invalidate marketplace observations.

### Gate 2 — Local liquidity

A concentrated operating area must demonstrate that passengers can encounter genuinely available supply and drivers can encounter meaningful demand.

### Gate 3 — Behavioral evidence

Registrations are insufficient. Repeat passenger usage, driver availability, acceptance and completed rides must show that participants choose to use the marketplace after initial acquisition.

### Gate 4 — Economic evidence

Observed revenue and realistic variable/operating costs must support a path toward positive contribution economics without permanent subsidy.

### Gate 5 — Expansion

Only after the earlier gates survive should the pilot expand geography, supply segments or demand channels.

## Public disclosure rule

Pilot metrics should remain private until they are verified and sufficiently representative. Any future public metric must state its period, population and definition so that prototype tests, registrations and real commercial activity cannot be confused.
